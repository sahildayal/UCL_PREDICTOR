The veteran scout

Dixon–Coles

The classic football model. Every club gets an attack and a defence rating, every league gets a strength, and goals follow a Poisson curve with a fix for low scores. The backbone two of the other models build on.

Testing: Club strength is league strength plus a shrunk club deviation; goals are Poisson with a low-score correction.

The professor

Bayesian

The same idea done fully Bayesian: club ratings sit inside league ratings, so a club with little European history borrows strength from its league until its own results say otherwise.

Testing: Club ratings are drawn from league-level distributions; integrating over rating uncertainty beats conditioning on its mode, especially for clubs with few observed matches.

The networker

Graph net

Treats every club as a point in a web of who has played whom, and passes strength along the links. Built for clubs that never meet each other at home.

Testing: Propagating club representations along the fixture graph captures cross-league strength better than a scalar rating.

The chess master

Elo

One number per club, nudged up or down after every match, like chess ratings. It is the control: if Elo keeps up, the fancier models aren't earning their keep.

Testing: A single rating per club, updated match by match, is enough.

The film analyst

Sequence

A neural network that reads each club's recent matches as a sequence. It only knows form, not who a club is, which is how it once gave Sabah a real chance against Manchester United.
